1What is Markowitz Mean-Variance Optimisation?

Nobel-prize idea, still the standard building block of quantitative asset allocation.

Harry Markowitz (1952) proposed that a rational investor should evaluate the portfolio as a whole, balancing expected return against variance. The optimiser finds the weight vector that maximises expected return for a chosen level of risk aversion.

The output is not a magic winning portfolio. It is the mathematically consistent starting point on which any subsequent overlay (macro judgement, AI model, tactical adjustment) can be layered. In this practicum, the AI overlay is that layer.

Three moving parts

  • μ · vector of expected returns per asset
  • Σ · covariance matrix of asset returns
  • λ · risk-aversion parameter

Output

  • w · vector of optimal weights (summing to 100 %)

β · Beta coefficients · asset systematic risk relative to the market

Beta is the classical MBA measure of how much an asset moves when the market moves. SPY is the market proxy here, so it has β = 1.00 by definition. Assets with β > 1 amplify market swings, β < 1 dampen them, β near zero move independently.

4The equation Markowitz solves

maxw   μT·w   −   (λ / 2) · wT·Σ·w
expected portfolio return minus half of risk-aversion times portfolio variance
subject to   Σᵢ wᵢ = 1   (weights sum to 100 %)   and   wᵢ ≥ 0   (long-only)

Analytical solution (unconstrained): w = (1/λ) · Σ⁻¹ · μ. With long-only and sum-to-one, we normalise and clip.

What is a market benchmark, and why does it matter?

A market benchmark is a reference portfolio against which any strategy is measured. The classic MBA-textbook benchmark is the 60/40 portfolio: 60% in stocks (like SPY) and 40% in bonds (like AGG). Vanguard sells this exact allocation as the VBIAX Balanced Index Fund at a cost of 0.07% per year · almost free.

The benchmark answers the toughest question in portfolio management: is my strategy actually adding value, or would I do just as well by buying a passive 60/40 index fund and going home? If the MVO recommendation cannot beat 60/40 in 2025, then all the estimation work was for nothing · a client could have paid Vanguard 0.07% and got a similar or better result.
